## Colorado elk by the numbers

Colorado issues more elk tags than any other western state, but the quality gap between units is enormous. A hunter willing to do their homework can find a genuinely exceptional hunt without waiting a decade.

Here's how to think about the Colorado elk draw in 2026.

## OTC (over-the-counter) vs. limited entry

Colorado sells unlimited archery elk tags statewide and rifle tags in many units over-the-counter. OTC units get more pressure, but they also mean you're hunting every year instead of sitting out.

For many hunters — especially those just starting their preference point journey — OTC units in Colorado's western slope elk country are an excellent option. Units like GMU 2, 3, and 13 offer large public land footprints with genuine trophy potential.

## 1–3 points: units to target

With just a few preference points, Colorado elk hunters can draw some solid limited-entry units. The key is finding units where pressure is moderate and the draw hasn't yet exploded from internet attention.

Target units where draw odds are 15–40% in your bracket. You're not guaranteed to draw, but the expected wait is reasonable. Run multiple choice selections — use your first choice for your dream unit and backup choices for realistic targets.

## 6–10 points: mid-tier opportunities open up

At this point range, some of Colorado's most productive rifle elk units become accessible. These aren't trophy-factory units, but they consistently produce 5x6 bulls and have excellent public land access.

The best plays in this range tend to be units that got popular 3–4 years ago (so competition was rising) but have now stabilized as hunters shifted focus elsewhere.

## 12–15+ points: premium tags within reach

This is where Colorado elk hunting transforms. Units that require 12+ points have limited pressure, genuine trophy genetics, and success rates that professional outfitters quote with confidence.

If you're in this point range, the single biggest mistake is waiting another year without a clear strategy. Use Scout Pro to model exactly when you're likely to draw your target unit and whether an adjacent unit with slightly lower point requirements might offer similar hunting quality at a shorter wait.

The hunters who consistently draw great Colorado elk tags aren't more patient — they're more strategic. They're applying to units they'll actually draw, not just putting in for the best unit every year and hoping.
